CHOOSE YOUR COMPARISON

New here? Start with these three steps.
  1. 01

    Read the borrowing amount.

    Recorded or book balance means loans and notes in the accounts. Contractual principal is the amount due under the debt terms, not necessarily cash due today. Discounts, fees and accounting adjustments can make the figures differ; leases are left out of this ranking.

  2. 02

    Put it beside assets.

    Assets include equipment, buildings and cash; they are not all spendable money. Debt / assets compares recorded borrowings with recorded assets. It does not show how easily a company can repay.

    Arithmetic example — not a company estimate$30 of recorded borrowings ÷ $100 of recorded assets = 30%.
  3. 03

    Treat coupons as note terms.

    A coupon is the stated interest rate on one note. Current market yield depends on its trading price and terms, so it can differ. Convertible notes can also give lenders rights to shares; a low coupon is not the whole financing cost.

02 / WHAT THESE NUMBERS CAN TELL YOU

Three measures. Three different questions.

01 / RECORDED AMOUNT

Book value isn't always principal.

The first ranking uses loan and note balances recorded in the accounts. Discounts and financing adjustments can make this different from the principal due under the debt terms. Open a company for its reconciliation and excluded leases. A larger balance alone does not show repayment ability.

02 / FINANCING MIX

A ratio adds context.

Debt divided by assets shows dollars of borrowings per $100 of recorded assets. It does not identify which assets were funded by debt. It is not debt divided by equity, a default probability, or a measure of cash available to pay interest.

03 / CONTRACT TERMS

A coupon is a contract.

A fixed coupon is the interest rate on one named note. It is not its current trading yield, the cost of every loan the company has, or the rate it would pay on new debt today.

How we handle the gaps

We use dated company disclosures and primary filings. Missing or non-comparable values stay unranked. Ranks and bar widths use the full selected peer group; search only hides rows, and ties share a rank. The table does not blend a new financing announcement into an older balance sheet.
